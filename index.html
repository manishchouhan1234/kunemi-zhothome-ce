An earned house

A HOUSE THAT IS EARNED

The Künemi Zhothome Ce is presented by IGRMS as the traditional house of a wealthy Chakhesang Naga who had earned the honour of hosting a community feast for the villagers. In the source account, the house is closely connected with achievement, honour, generosity and social recognition.

The house was originally built in Chozuba village by the grandfather of the family. In 2003, people from Chozuba came together to recreate it at IGRMS, bringing traditional heirlooms and household objects with it.

“The house of this kind is not simply built, it is earned.— translated wording from the IGRMS source

Look up before we look in

LOOK UP BEFORE WE LOOK IN

Two horn-like projections rise from the roof. The IGRMS source calls these Ceka (horns) and describes them as announcing the accomplishments of the family to the village. The horns and carvings are presented as part of a visual narrative of identity and achievement.

The Ceka rise above the roof as a visible sign of the family’s accomplishments. The source places them within the wider architectural language of the house, where carvings and structural elements communicate honour, beliefs and identity.

Architecture as a visual record

READING THE HOUSE

The IGRMS source describes the architectural features as meaningful rather than merely decorative. Together, the carvings, skulls, figures, pillars and horns form a record of family achievement, cultural beliefs and identity.

Wealth as contribution

THE RICHMAN

In the IGRMS account, wealth is valued not simply for what it accumulates, but for what it contributes. A family with sufficient agricultural harvest and livestock may host a Feast of Merit, described as a major expression of generosity and social standing.

The whole village is invited to participate. Hosting successive feasts brings respect and recognition; after the fourth feast, the source says that villagers erect large commemorative stones in recognition of the host’s generosity and service.

The source also records that in Chozuba one individual who hosted the feast eight times was remembered through 28 commemorative stones.

Practical life and inherited objects

INSIDE THE HOUSE

The interior described by IGRMS combines everyday functions with features and heirlooms that carry the family’s traditions and achievements. The house has no windows; its low thatched roof extends almost to the ground, and a central hearth provides warmth.

A journey of reconstruction

FROM A NAGA VILLAGE TO THE MUSEUM

01

TRADITIONAL CHOZUBA

The house belonged to a tradition associated with Chozuba village. The source says nearly 300 such houses once stood there, with seven remaining at the time of the source account.

02

FAMILY & COMMUNITY

The house is connected with the Chakhesang Richman and Feast of Merit tradition, with family heirlooms carrying memories of earlier generations.

03

2003

People of Chozuba came together to reconstruct the house at IGRMS, travelling from Nagaland and raising it using traditional building techniques.

04

TODAY

At IGRMS, the reconstructed house presents an account of Chakhesang architectural and cultural traditions through the building and associated heirlooms.
